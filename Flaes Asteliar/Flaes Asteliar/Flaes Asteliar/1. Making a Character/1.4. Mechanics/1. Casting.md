Casting is divided into three stages: declaration, cast action, and resolution.

Declaration occurs when a caster attempts to cast a spell. It happens simultaneously with the actual speed of that spell, whether it be an action, bonus action, or otherwise. All spells, unless specified otherwise, require both vocal and somatic commitment. During this stage, the caster must select the spell to be used, pay the mana cost of the spell, then select any targets that the spell requires.

If there is a casting delay above 0, the caster must then enter a cast action, during which they will incur an action delay equal to casting delay, then immediately end their turn. They must remain in their cast action until the start of their next turn. While they are in the middle of a cast action cannot take reactions unless they choose to break their cast action. If they take damage, they must immediately succeed a DC 10 concentration save or break their casting action.

Once a cast action has fully completed and the caster's next turn has arrived, the caster at the start of their turn can proceed with resolution or choose to break their cast action. If there was no cast action necessary, resolution will simply occur immediately after declaration.

During resolution, the caster's spell will fully manifest and any effects that trigger upon a target being targeted will be triggered. The caster will immediately resolve the effects of their spell if they are not interrupted, then incur an action delay equal to the action delay of the spell. They are free to resolve the rest of their turn as they please.

If a spell requires concentration, you cannot attempt another concentration spell while that spell is active. Taking damage while concentrating on a spell forces you to make a DC 10 concentration save.
