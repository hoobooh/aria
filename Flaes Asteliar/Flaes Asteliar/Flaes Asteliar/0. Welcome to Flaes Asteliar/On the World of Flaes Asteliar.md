	The brightest flame.

Flaes Asteliar refers to the world that Aria5e is primarily set in. The term *Flaes Asteliar* comes from a long lost language from the previous civilization that once inhabited this world and means "the brightest flame." While technically its full name, many people also refer to the world as simply *Asteliar.*

Asteliar has several key characteristics that distinguish it from other worlds.

### Abysses

The seas are massive and possess even deeper "Abysses" where even the water plummets into an endless fog from which wanderers don't return. Sailors must take care not to go astray of their routes and get swallowed up by an Abyss, while different countries take great care to keep an eye on the mysterious creatures that occasionally claw their way up from those depths. Ships should also take great care to observe the weather, as Abysses will rarely produce massive whirlpools called Abyss Storms that suck in everything around them violently.

The biggest Abyss in the world is undoubtedly the Endless Abyss, which covers most of the world. A few Abysses are actually present inside continents; most notable of which would be the Heart's Abyss within Primera.

Abysses are believed to accumulate and release magic into the rest of the world, and as such play a vital part in the natural order despite their destructive characteristics. It is unclear what lies beneath the dense fog inside them; nothing that goes so far down ever comes back up. The only hint as to their innerworkings lies in the Abyssals, mysterious creatures that crawl out from under the depths and lurk the ocean borders.

The magical release of Abysses affect their environments in drastic ways. Most visible are the megaliths, which float in the air despite their massive size. The fog obscuring the Abysses breaks down low-level enchantments and runes— even going as far as to permanently damage the magic capabilities of a creature if they're left in it for too long. Some theorize that the fog is created by a dense floor of hot liquid magic that instantly evaporates the never-ending downpour of water from the oceans.
### Megaliths

Even more curious are the giant floating islands that rest above these sinister Abysses. Called megaliths in some circles, they vary in danger and are largely thought to float by circulating energy from their adjacent Abyss.
Due to the wide expanse of even a single Abyss, it is believed that only a few megaliths have even been discovered by modern civilization. Of the ones known to civilization, the most well known is without a doubt the massive archipelago that shelters Judicia, a Starborn empire which inherited the abandoned remnants of a civilization called Justitia.
### The World Spire

One subject of great interest and research within Flaes Asteliar is the World Spire, a colossal tower from the Forgotten Era which remains unfinished. It's visible from nearly everywhere in the continent of Primera and resides on what is commonly called the Heart megalith, located in a gigantic Abyss in the middle of the continent. It is also the only known structure that seems to extend down into an Abyss itself.

The original purpose and origin of the World Spire is a hotly debated topic. Some believe it to be a religious monument, while others argue it was a weapon of great destruction. The Spire Extinction Theory suggests that the World Spire's main functions have already been used, annihilating Asteliar's ancient civilizations and ending the Forgotten Era.

Regardless of its original purposes, the region around the Spire remains extremely dangerous to approach. Ancient constructs and corrupted Centurials roam the megalith's shattered plains, along with swarms of mechanical wyvern constructs that patrol the foggy expanse of the Abyss. Combined with the frequent Abyss storms surrounding the megalith, the World Spire is simply not a viable target for active exploration.

Despite its many dangers, the World Spire has garnered quite a gathering of worshippers. Every year, those who live on the inner coasts of Primera report pilgrims embarking on one-way journey into the Heart seeking to become closer to the target of their worship.
### Continents

Continents in Flaes Asteliar are noticeably small. Most only hold 1 country at most, aside from Primera and Reis which both hold two each. Asterliaran continents are defined by the Abyss(s) around them, which group off different landmasses together. Much of the land within each remains uninhabited due to a lack of easily accessible resources and roaming magical beasts that have slowly been increasing in number.

Each continent is also noticeably far apart. Combined with the need to travel through or above Abyss fog, inter-continent travel is rather rare and usually reserved for military or wealthy individuals. As an upside, however, wars between countries are extremely unlikely. Anthropologists of various countries have all noted that the level of peace currently being experienced in the world is unlike any other point in history that they've managed to uncover.

It is fairly certain that the continents are not moving— a concern sometimes voiced by commoners upon observing the megaliths' tendency to slightly shift from time to time.
### Magic

Magic, despite the active Abysses which constantly spew it out, is quite weak when harnessed in the hands of creatures. Barring a few beasts and people, most who pursue the magical arts can barely manage to conjure a fireball by after copious training. Those without magical aptitude would be hard-pressed to manage so much as a single cantrip.

Magic, known in academic circles as Aulera, grows within people in all sorts of ways. Some train their entire lives trying to harness the energy that infuses itself into reality, studying theory at academies and seeking out mentors. Others simply chance into it; whether it be inherent talent that they never checked, a strong trauma changing them on a fundamental level, or sometimes even pure luck.

By consequence, the known world within Flaes Asteliar is fairly lacking in proper magic users. Artificers are much more prevalent and are a staple of any magic-dependent industry. While any large city possesses sophisticated runed machinery, actual magic users, commonly known as Astella(s), are always in higher demand than supply can satisfy. Justitia in particular has been an active player in developing magic academies, its capital becoming known in the world as a prestigious citadel of knowledge where aspiring students flock to.
### Technology / Knowledge

Flaes Asteliar is quite advanced in some aspects and quite behind in others. Wealthy cities have access to cutting edge artificer machinery that can fly, move as though they have their own minds, and perform complicated tasks.

Other things, however, appear rather lacking. Factories as a concept aren't very sustainable due to the high maintenance costs of artificer technology. Medicine relies heavily on traditional medicine and  and, for those wealthy enough to seek it, alchemists. Political systems are largely structured around monarchies. Formal education is only plentiful for those with the coin to afford it. Farms are always under threat of magical beast attacks and natural disasters.

Much of the advancements and sophisticated technology the world of Asteliar has access to are actually derived from the Forgotten Era, which was substantially more advanced. Most academies depend on scavengers to dig through ancient ruins in search of more records or technology.
### Transportation / Airships

In a world with hazards like Abyss fog, transportation has welcomed a new type of travel. Airships are an extremely common sight for anyone moving between continents or even between cities, as the wilderness can be full of extremely dangerous magical beasts. Ranging from tiny 1-person vessels to staggering cruise ships meant to carry entire cities if necessary, artificer technology is the backbone of modern transportation in Asteliar.

Theories have been made regarding turning megaliths into giant flying vessels, but many fear that disturbing the floating islands too much may break down the inner magic that lets them float in the first place. No countries are willing to experiment with the valuable refuges that airships seek above the Abyss fog.

This isn't to say that traditional ships no longer exist; sailing in the oceans is certainly much cheaper and non-magical ships tend to require quite a bit less maintenance. Regular ships are still frequently used within oceans; particularly within Primera.

Whether the vessel be traditional or artificer-made, all ships rest at seaside harbors built to receive both types. Gigantic stations welcome all sorts of vessels into their bays. These harbors are also flocking points for artificers, who won't be lacking for well-paid work in such places. Poorer or smaller towns often only have mundane docks due to the high cost of building sophisticated stations.

To the northeast, those in Reis are rarely spotted riding tamed wyverns as well. The art of doing so is highly coveted and only taught to the most important people in the continent, though such a thing isn't in high demand with airships being so much safer and convenient.
### Military / Conflict

Each country possesses a military, though their policies and doctrines tend to differ. Generally, Asteliaran militaries are not particularly oppressive with the exception of Judicia, which maintains a specialized Inquisition to handle internal manners. Militaries are comprised of mostly non-Astellas wielding swords, spears, and bows. Some branches of Arkadian military also use runed muskets, but they're both expensive and difficult to reload. Forian military forces employ special constructs created through intricate artificing. 

A war between countries would be both brutal and slow. Military air cruisers are substantially slower than smaller vehicles, making large scale offenses extremely vulnerable to defensive maneuvers no matter what approach is taken. Once two sides have engaged in a large-scale battle, the lack of special firepower will make the act of killing arduous and painful.

As war is so difficult to even initiate, militaries mostly exist as politics and to maintain internal order. They're only called to mobilize when a crisis such as a magical beast swarm or a growing cult occurs. Consequently, joining the military is surprisingly popular.

Aside from militaries, countries also employ guards to maintain public order as well as mercenaries. Mercenaries are a somewhat common sight in Asteliar— those with aptitude for fighting may find that it is beneficial to join a mercenary group and hunt for bounties or take on various combat jobs such as escorting. While not looked upon particularly favorably, most mercenary groups aren't particularly noteworthy in terms of morality.

### Religion

Beliefs in Flaes Asteliar are quite varied, though most seem to dislike the idea of gods all together. Divine beings of any kind haven't been seen in the modern era whatsoever. While the concept of such a thing certainly exists, modern records indicate that such beings simply don't exist in Flaes Asteliar.

Ruins and deciphered records from the Forgotten Era indicate that divine intervention used to be quite common; so common, in fact, that the Forgotten Era eventually ended with a brutal war against the gods that wiped almost everyone out. It's unclear who won, and it's even more unclear who these gods even were— as their first intervention was soon followed by the Celeste Destia: the War against the Stars.

Many believe that gods are what brought upon the downfall of a civilization that was observably much more advanced and capable than they were. Similar tales are passed down within cultures all around the world, though they're hardly more than folk tales told to children. Fairy tales warn of gods possessing people, gods deceiving people into giving their souls, and even gods wandering the untamed lands looking for people to devour. Strangely, very few gods from the Forgotten Era's records even have known names.

Despite the stigma against gods and the aforementioned gods' absence, certain communities still choose to worship various deities and figures. The most commons ones include the World Spire itself, the Red Star, and the few gods whose names were deciphered from ancient texts. Some villages in Reis worship Renga, the founder of both Rue and Rakir that some believe ascended to godhood following his death. Judicia worships the concept of Fate, though not quite as a deity.

While religious worship isn't explicitly banned, it's looked upon unfavorably in almost every city outside of those in Reis, where it's merely tolerated.
### Astrology

Last but most importantly, this world is illuminated by the most central subject of them all: the Red Star.

The Red Star is a bright red star in the sky, as well as Flaes Asteliar's only visible star at night. It's unclear *why* it's the only star in the sky; ancient records indicate that there used to be a wide expanse of stars every night. Interestingly, the first record of the Red Star and the disappearance of all other stars seem to coincide. The Red Star rises in the east and sets in the west every night. Comparing old drawings of stars to the Red Star, it also appears to be substantially bigger and brighter than its peers.

The starless night sky also hosts two moons. The first moon, Lyla, is significantly bigger than its counterpart, Laos. Researchers theorize that the two moons were originally one but something split them apart. It is believed that the two moons are responsible for regulating magic flow in the world.

During the day, a bright sun shines down onto the world. It has no particular name other than "the sun." Some cultures believe that it will one day wake up and consume the world, but this is largely dismissed by most people. It is generally understood that the sun is responsible for cultivating magic and purifying it.

Other phenomena include auroras, which frequently flow through the skies all over the world. It's unclear how far the sky goes up and what lies beyond it; airships seem to break down past a certain altitude.
